Coming Soon · In testing on Solana devnet Invoice Tokenization

Your invoices,
as tokens anyone can check.

Tokenizing an invoice your customer still owes mints tokens for what's outstanding into your business's wallet. Each token stands for one dollar owed, and burns as it's paid.

One token per dollar owed

Each business has its own US dollar receivables token. Its supply always equals what's still owed on the business's tokenized invoices, so a token from one business is never mixed with another's.

Burns as invoices are paid

A payment, credit or write-off burns the same number of tokens. A tokenized invoice is locked against edits until its last token burns, and a change in QuickBooks is held back and disclosed rather than applied silently.

On Solana, with nothing private on-chain

Every mint and burn is recorded on-chain with a fingerprint of the invoice behind it. Nothing on-chain names the business, its customers or its invoices.

Minted to your wallet

Tokens go to a wallet your business proved it controls. They move only between accounts the platform allows, and Scriply, as the issuer, burns them as invoices are paid.

Opt-in, per business

An admin turns tokenizing on after the business is verified: they accept the tokenization terms and choose the wallet. Nothing goes on-chain before that.

A limit on what's out at once

Each business has a cap on how much it can have tokenized at a time. A tokenization over it is refused before anything is minted.

What we check before an invoice is tokenized.

A token is only worth the invoice behind it. Every business and every invoice goes through these steps first.

1

We verify the business

A business passes our review before it can tokenize: its owners with a 25% stake or more and a person who controls it are identified, and the business and its people are screened against sanctions lists.

2

We verify the wallet

Tokens are only minted to a wallet the business proves it controls, by signing with it.

3

We check every invoice

Each invoice needs a supporting document and a customer who clears sanctions screening. It's compared with every invoice already tokenized, by any business: the same invoice is refused, and one that looks alike waits for a person to review it. An invoice from QuickBooks is read again there before anything is minted.

4

We report on every token, publicly

Every day we publish each token's report — what's outstanding, how overdue, how concentrated, how fast it's paid, and what we verified — with its fingerprint recorded on-chain.

Illustrative report for a made-up business. Units are dollars of invoices still owed.

supply · outstandingUnits · reconciled
Tokens in existence, and dollars still owed on the invoices behind them. They match whenever the report reconciles.
burnDebt
Tokens owed for burning that couldn't be burned yet. While it's above zero the token is under-backed, the report says so, and the business can't tokenize anything new.
aging
What's owed, by days past due: current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and over 90.
risk
How concentrated the customers are, the share of invoices made in Scriply rather than an accounting system, how fast invoices are paid, and flags from a fixed list, such as concentration-high or aging-over-90-high.
assurance
What we verified about the business behind the token, such as its EIN and registration, its owners and its wallet, without saying who it is.
sha256 · attestations
The fingerprint of the day's full report, and the Solana transaction that recorded it. A lender given the full report hashes it and compares it with the chain.
What the report doesn't promise. We stand behind how each token is made: who could tokenize, what was checked, and that supply matches what's owed. We don't promise that customers will pay or how good the invoices are as credit. Those are for each lender to judge, and the report is there so they can.
